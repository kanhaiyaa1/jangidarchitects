Building Plan Approval in Mumbai: IOD/IOA, CC and OC Explained

Building Approvals · 1 November 2026 · 5 min read

Every new building or redevelopment project in Mumbai needs approval from the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) before work starts and again after it ends. Three documents decide whether a project moves ahead on time: the IOD/IOA, the CC and the OC.

What is building plan approval in Mumbai

Building plan approval is the formal permission to construct a building on a plot. The plan must follow the Development Control and Promotion Regulations (DCPR 2034) and other rules that apply to the site. Approval is not a single step. It is a sequence of permissions, issued by the planning authority as the project moves from drawing board to handover.

Intimation of Disapproval or Approval (IOD/IOA)

The IOD/IOA is the first formal response from BMC after it scrutinises the submitted plan. Despite the name, it is not a rejection. It is a letter that lists the conditions the owner or developer must meet before construction can begin, such as clearances from other departments, payment of premiums and charges, and corrections to the drawings.

An IOD/IOA does not allow construction to start. It tells you exactly what remains to be done.

Commencement Certificate (CC)

Once the conditions in the IOD/IOA are complied with, BMC issues the Commencement Certificate. The CC is the permission to start construction. On larger projects it is often released in stages, for example up to the plinth first and then for the upper floors, so work in each stage can proceed only after the earlier one is cleared.

Occupancy Certificate (OC)

After the building is complete, the owner applies for the Occupancy Certificate. The OC confirms that the building has been built as per the approved plans and is fit for use. Residents cannot legally occupy the building without it, and it is usually needed for permanent water and electricity connections and for the formation of a society.

The approval process step by step

  1. Study the plot, its ownership documents and the development rules that apply to it.
  2. Prepare the architectural plans as per DCPR 2034 and submit the proposal to BMC.
  3. Respond to scrutiny remarks from the different BMC departments.
  4. Receive the IOD/IOA and comply with each condition, including the required No Objection Certificates (NOCs).
  5. Receive the CC and begin construction.
  6. Obtain stage clearances and inspections as the work progresses.
  7. Apply for the OC on completion and hand over the building.

Documents usually needed

  • Ownership documents, property card and survey details of the plot
  • Architect's drawings and area statements
  • Structural design and reports by a licensed structural engineer
  • NOCs from the departments that apply to the project
  • Society or developer resolutions and consents, in the case of redevelopment

The exact list depends on the plot, the authority and the type of project, so it should be confirmed at the start.

Why approvals get delayed

  • Incomplete or incorrect drawings that lead to repeated remarks
  • Pending NOCs from other departments
  • Unresolved title, tenancy or society issues
  • Plans that do not match the development rules
  • Slow follow-up between the submission and the issue of each document

Frequently asked questions

Is an IOD/IOA the same as plan approval?

No. The IOD/IOA is the first stage. It lists the conditions to be met. Construction can begin only after those conditions are complied with and the CC is issued.

What is the difference between CC and OC?

The CC allows construction to start. The OC is issued after construction is complete and confirms the building can be occupied.

Can residents move in without an OC?

No. A building should not be occupied without an Occupancy Certificate. Without it, permanent utility connections and other formalities can also be held up.
